Trustworthy Numerical Software in the Age of AI: What Would Turing Do?

Summary

AI coding agents now produce numerical software faster than experts can review it. This raises concerns about the reliability of that software, and it motivates our first aim, assessing AI-generated numerical kernels against accuracy requirements. We audit a public GPU QR competition. The winning kernel ran 107 times faster than the baseline built on NVIDIA's routine. All five leading kernels passed the competition's accuracy tests, yet each failed at least once on new inputs from the competition's generator under the same checker, while the baseline passed them all. Our second aim is to develop reusable formalizations of numerical analysis, which remains sparsely represented in existing formal libraries. This gap motivates VSCL, our Lean library of numerical-analysis results. VERNA is designed to connect the two aims by using formalized results to guide the assessment of kernels against explicit specifications.

What the paper contains

The paper is organized as follows.

  • Section 1 introduces the paper. It gives the audit as a cautionary example, describes the limited coverage of numerical analysis in formal libraries, and states the paper's contributions.
  • Section 2 presents the audit.
  • Section 3 presents VSCL, its mathematical content, the reuse analysis, and the formalization workflow, and builds on the library with a case study of a survey of randomized numerical linear algebra.
  • Section 4 introduces VERNA.
  • Section 5 sets out formalization targets in stability analysis and discusses the path from numerical methods to mathematical software, including underflow, exceptional values, and compiled code. It then turns to the cost of formalizing at scale and closes with seven design goals under the question "What would Turing do?".
  • Appendix A surveys existing guarantees for numerical algorithms and programs, their floating-point foundations, and AI-assisted formalization.
  • Appendices B, C and D give the details of the audit, the reuse analysis, and the experiments with open-weight models.
