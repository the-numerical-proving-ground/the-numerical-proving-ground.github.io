The RandNLA case study in VSCL: statements and proofs

Browse the statements · RandNLA definitions

Lean has checked proofs of the spectral-norm bound for element-wise sparsification, the structural bound for sketched low-rank approximation, the subspace embedding property and the relative-error least-squares bound of the subsampled randomized Hadamard transform, a sparse subspace embedding, and the relative-error least-squares bound with approximate leverage-score sampling, all in exact arithmetic. The bound for sparsification, the two results for the subsampled randomized Hadamard transform and the least-squares bound with leverage-score sampling rest on Lieb's concavity theorem, which the library proves. These results come from the survey by Drineas and Mahoney, RandNLA: Randomized Numerical Linear Algebra, Communications of the ACM 59(6), 2016, which this case study formalizes in VSCL. Lean reports that all 35 proofs use no axiom beyond its three standard ones (propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound). Our paper discusses this case study.

Each statement is published apart from its proof, so that a reader can compare it with the survey without reading any proof. No expert outside our group has been asked to make this comparison. At commit 55a62f4 of 2026-11-16 the library publishes 47 statements from the survey, and 35 of them have a published proof. The subspace embedding for leverage-score sampling is among the 12 statements whose proof is not published.

The sparsification bound is proved for square matrices and a thresholded sampler. The low-rank bound adds a condition on the singular vectors and bounds the error rather than its square, which is weaker for the spectral and Frobenius norms. The sparse embedding, the two Hadamard results and the leverage-score least-squares bound fix choices that the survey leaves open. The two results for the subsampled randomized Hadamard transform take the number of rows to be a power of two, and no statement concerns running time. The library's notes on the groups of statements record each choice and change.

Proof-free contracts for subspace invariance and geometry

BasisGeometry.lean · 2 statements, each with a published proof

CACM 59(6), p. 85, Eq. (6) and the prose after Eq. (7). These targets isolate basis independence and the deterministic consequences of an embedding event; they do not replace the separate randomized Eq. (7) construction.

  • Equation (6), page 85

    Leverage probabilities are independent of the chosen basis

    The normalized leverage law depends only on the subspace. If orthonormal-column matrices U,VU,V span the same positive-dimensional space, their projectors UUTUU^{\mathsf T} and VVTVV^{\mathsf T} agree, as do their leverage scores and probabilities.

    Definitions used (3)
    Orthonormal columns
    A real matrix UU has orthonormal columns if each column has Euclidean length one and distinct columns have zero inner product. Equivalently, UTU=IU^{\mathsf T}U=I.
    Leverage score
    Given a matrix UU whose columns form an orthonormal basis of a subspace, row ii has leverage score ℓi=∥Ui,:∥22=(UUT)ii\ell_i=\|U_{i,:}\|_2^2=(UU^{\mathsf T})_{ii}.
    Leverage sampling probability
    For an orthonormal basis UU of positive width dd, the leverage probability of row ii is pi=ℓi/dp_i=\ell_i/d. The row scores sum to dd.

    Uses HasOrthonormalColumns, leverageScore, leverageScoreProb

    Library note

    The normalized leverage law is independent of the orthonormal basis chosen for the same positive-dimensional column space. Frame widths may be supplied independently; orthonormality and equal ranges force consistency.

    Lean statement
    def leverageBasisInvariance : Prop :=
  ∀ (m n p : ℕ) (U : Matrix (Fin m) (Fin n) ℝ)
    (V : Matrix (Fin m) (Fin p) ℝ),
    0 < n → 0 < p → HasOrthonormalColumns U → HasOrthonormalColumns V →
    Set.range U.mulVec = Set.range V.mulVec →
      (U * U.transpose = V * V.transpose) ∧
      (∀ i, leverageScore U i = leverageScore V i) ∧
      (∀ i, leverageScoreProb U i = leverageScoreProb V i)

  • Equation (7), page 85, and the text after it

    Embedding geometry preserves norms and rank on the subspace

    Under Equation (7) with 0<ε<10<\varepsilon<1, the sketch SS preserves squared lengths within factors 1±ε1\pm\varepsilon throughout the specified column space. Covered-support entry-residual moment contract

EntryResidualMoments.lean · 1 statement, with a published proof

Original row016, p83, concerns zero mean and bounded variance of entries of the raw Algorithm1 residual. This conditional contract explicitly retains the nonzero-entry support coverage required by corrected unbiasedness. It does not silently repair the original unrestricted claim. The supplied distribution is normalized by its finite-probability type, and draws use its literal iid law. No truncation or independence between different residual coordinates is asserted.

Corrected, thresholded entry-sparsification contract

EntrySparsification.lean · 1 statement, with a published proof

This is the square theorem supported by Drineas--Zouzias (survey reference18), with its explicit zero branch and upward-compatible integer sample condition. It is a correction to applying survey Equation(2) to the unchanged raw sampler. The strict-threshold convention is the printed algorithm-box convention.

  • Equation (2), in the corrected form of the survey's reference 18

    A spectral error bound for thresholded entry sparsification

    For a square n×nn\times n matrix AA, set to zero every entry of magnitude less than ε/(2n)\varepsilon/(2n). Draw ss independent samples from the remaining entries with probabilities proportional to their squares. If 14nlog⁡(2n/δ) ∥A∥F2≤sε2,14n\log(2n/\delta)\,\|A\|_{\mathrm F}^2\le s\varepsilon^2, then with probability at least 1−δ1-\delta the spectral error is at most ε\varepsilon, with at most ss nonzero entries in the sketch. This is the thresholded form of the survey’s reference 18. Equation (2) describes the sampler without the threshold.

Equation (7) as a success criterion

GramEmbeddingCriterion.lean · 1 statement, with a published proof

This is a condition on an arbitrary sketch, not a claim that a particular randomized construction succeeds. Its quadratic criterion holds simultaneously for every coefficient vector and includes zero coefficient dimension.

Leading-subspace leverage distribution

LeadingSubspaceLeverage.lean · 1 statement, with a published proof

The footnote-e variant of equation (6) selects the first k left singular vectors of a supplied compatible ordered SVD. Thus the matrix A and the selected leading subspace are linked by reconstruction, rank and orthonormality, rather than by an unspecified leading-subspace predicate. Choices at a repeated singular value are fixed by the supplied SVD. An arbitrary orthonormal basis of that fixed subspace is allowed, specified by equality of actual matrix-vector image sets. Rank-deficient inputs are allowed.

  • Page 87, the least-squares sketch, with approximate leverage scores

    Building the approximate-leverage least-squares sketch

    Normalize the supplied relative scores into probabilities and sketch AA and bb using the same independently sampled rows. The construction covers the zero-rank case separately.
