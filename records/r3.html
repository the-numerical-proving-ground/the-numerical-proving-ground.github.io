The RandNLA case study in VSCL: statements and proofs

Lean has checked proofs of the spectral-norm bound for element-wise sparsification, the structural bound for sketched low-rank approximation, the subspace embedding property and the relative-error least-squares bound of the subsampled randomized Hadamard transform, a sparse subspace embedding, and the relative-error least-squares bound with approximate leverage-score sampling, all in exact arithmetic. The bound for sparsification, the two results for the subsampled randomized Hadamard transform and the least-squares bound with leverage-score sampling rest on Lieb's concavity theorem, which the library proves. These results come from the survey by Drineas and Mahoney, RandNLA: Randomized Numerical Linear Algebra, Communications of the ACM 59(6), 2016, which this case study formalizes in VSCL. Lean reports that all 35 proofs use no axiom beyond its three standard ones (propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound). Our paper discusses this case study.

Each statement is published apart from its proof, so that a reader can compare it with the survey without reading any proof. No expert outside our group has been asked to make this comparison. At commit 55a62f4 of 2026-11-16 the library publishes 47 statements from the survey, and 35 of them have a published proof. The subspace embedding for leverage-score sampling is among the 12 statements whose proof is not published.

The sparsification bound is proved for square matrices and a thresholded sampler. The low-rank bound adds a condition on the singular vectors and bounds the error rather than its square, which is weaker for the spectral and Frobenius norms. The sparse embedding, the two Hadamard results and the leverage-score least-squares bound fix choices that the survey leaves open. The two results for the subsampled randomized Hadamard transform take the number of rows to be a power of two, and no statement concerns running time. The library's notes on the groups of statements record each choice and change.

EntryResidualMoments.lean · 1 statement, with a published proof

Original row016, p83, concerns zero mean and bounded variance of entries of the raw Algorithm1 residual. Corrected, thresholded entry-sparsification contract

EntrySparsification.lean · 1 statement, with a published proof

This is the square theorem supported by Drineas--Zouzias (survey reference18), with its explicit zero branch and upward-compatible integer sample condition. It is a correction to applying survey Equation(2) to the unchanged raw sampler. The strict-threshold convention is the printed algorithm-box convention.

  • correctedTruncatedEntryGuarantee

    In plain words: For a square n × n matrix A, the entries smaller than ε/(2n) in absolute value are set to zero, and s samples are drawn independently from the remaining entries, with probabilities proportional to their squares. If 14 n log(2n/δ) times the squared Frobenius norm of A is at most s ε², then with probability at least 1 − δ the error in the spectral norm is at most ε and the sketch has at most s nonzero entries. The survey states Equation (2) for the sampler without this threshold, and this is the thresholded form of its reference 18.

    Corrected reference18 guarantee, including all matrices and at most s nonzero entries. The sample condition is multiplied by the positive eps².

    In the survey: Equation (2), in the corrected form of the survey's reference 18.

    Uses: ElementwiseSample, ElementwiseTrace, FiniteProbability.eventProb, frobNormSqRect, rectOpNorm2Le, truncatedElementwiseProbability, truncatedElementwiseSketch

Leading-subspace leverage distribution

LeadingSubspaceLeverage.lean · 1 statement, with a published proof

The footnote-e variant of equation (6) selects the first k left singular vectors of a supplied compatible ordered SVD. Thus the matrix A and the selected leading subspace are linked by reconstruction, rank and orthonormality, rather than by an unspecified leading-subspace predicate. Choices at a repeated singular value are fixed by the supplied SVD. An arbitrary orthonormal basis of that fixed subspace is allowed, specified by equality of actual matrix-vector image sets. Rank-deficient inputs are allowed.
